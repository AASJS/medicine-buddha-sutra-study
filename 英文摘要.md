## 1. Source Text

The repository is built on the CBETA collated text of the *Foshuo Yaoshi Rulai Benyuan Jing* （《佛說藥師如來本願經》）, translated by **Dharmagupta** （達磨笈多， Sui dynasty, 616 CE) — the **earliest** of the five extant Chinese translations of the Medicine Buddha sutra, and the one closest to the Indian recension. The later, far more widespread translation by **Xuanzang** （玄奘， T14n0450) adds several later-developed elements (the eight great bodhisattvas, the twelve-generals dhāraṇī) that Dharmagupta's version lacks.

## 3. The Twelve Vows (Overview)

The heart of the sutra is the twelve vows Bhaiṣajyaguru made while still a bodhisattva, all directed at worldly and transmundane fulfillment: radiance surpassing the sun and moon (1), a body like lapis lazuli (2), inexhaustible resources (3), establishing the stray on the path of awakening (4), unimpaired precepts (5), restoration of impaired faculties (6), healing of the sick and destitute (7), liberation of women in distress (8), release from heretical nets (9), relief of those bound and bereaved (10), food first and Dharma after (11), and clothing and adornments to the content of one's heart (12).

## 4. Findings: Dharmagupta vs. Xuanzang

Both translations render the same Indian recension: the number, order, and purport of the twelve vows are identical, and the differences are of wording, not doctrine. Five recurrent patterns (detailed in the comparison table):

1. **Systematic lexical replacement** — Xuanzang renders *sattva* as 有情 where Dharmagupta has 众生； likewise 异道→邪道， 辟支佛→独觉， 安立→安住， 花→华.
2. **Syntactic regularization** — looser phrasing is recast into symmetric four-character formulas and fixed liturgical compounds.
3. **Added dedicatory closures** — Xuanzang appends explicit restorative formulas ("should they commit any breach, hearing my name they shall again be purified"), completing each vow's *hearing → liberation → buddhahood* arc.
4. **Selection of detail** — Dharmagupta occasionally preserves older, more concrete particulars that Xuanzang condenses.
5. **Numbering style** — Dharmagupta's 十一大愿 / 十二大愿 (without 第) vs. Xuanzang's modern 第十一 / 第十二.
